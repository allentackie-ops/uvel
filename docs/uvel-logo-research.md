# Uvel logo research and recommendation

## What the research changes

The previous attempts failed because they jumped straight to visual output. They produced literal objects, generic geometric icons, or decorative concept art without first finding one strong idea that could own the brand.

Adobe’s brand-identity guidance is unusually direct: a logo should be **simple, distinctive, and relevant**, and should be reduced to basic shapes and one or two colors so it can work from mobile screens to print. It also recommends studying competitors before choosing the visual territory. [1]

Canva’s brand-identity guidance makes the same point in slightly different terms: the logo is the most recognizable part of the identity, so it needs to be simple, unique, and memorable. It also notes that the logo and color system have to work together as one recognizable image, not as unrelated decoration. [2]

Pentagram’s Mastercard case study is the useful professional example. The designers did not add more detail to make the mark feel premium. They isolated the existing idea to its purest forms, tested the colors across real contexts, and built a system that worked on cards, retail signs, websites, and phones. Pentagram reports that 81% of consumers spontaneously recognized the simplified symbol without the wordmark in its research. [3]

Apple’s identity guidelines show the operational side of the same principle: use the mark in a clean one-color treatment, protect clear space, avoid effects, and define a minimum size. The mark has to survive without presentation tricks. [4]

Rob Janoff’s interview about Apple adds the strategic lesson that matters most here. He says the bite made the fruit identifiable at a glance, gave the audience a small “wink,” and kept the mark simple enough to remember. His advice was to avoid adding every possible meaning and instead communicate one singular idea. [5]

Academic research also cautions against assuming that a literal logo is automatically better. A 2023 study found that figurative marks can improve recognition and associations, but their effect depends on how well the mark interacts with the brand name. A literal symbol that creates the wrong association can hurt recall. [6]

## What this means for Uvel

Uvel is an abstract name, so a fruit or object can give it a concrete memory hook. But the object cannot be random. It needs one visual behavior that makes it ownable. A plain fruit would be forgettable. A bitten apple-like fruit would invite comparison with Apple. The opportunity is a **simple organic pebble/seed form with a Uvel-specific cut**, not an Apple-style bite.

The cut should be a designed interruption rather than a round bite copied from another brand. It could be a shallow, off-center notch or a crisp slice that creates a recognizable silhouette. The mark should still read as one solid shape when filled black. The “wink” is the unexpected cut, not a stem, leaf, face, sparkle, or extra symbol.

## Recommended direction: the Uvel notch

**Brand sentence:** Uvel is the place where a personal find becomes part of your style.

**Visual idea:** a single rounded pebble/seed mark with one asymmetric notch. The pebble suggests something selected and kept. The notch makes it ownable and creates the memory hook. It is abstract enough to become a brand, but organic enough to feel human rather than corporate.

**Construction:** one filled organic silhouette; one cut only; no interior lines; no leaf; no stem; no shadow; no outline; no wordmark inside the icon. The notch should be asymmetric and positioned so the silhouette is recognizable even in one color.

**Color:** use Uvel’s dark olive as the field, `#2A320E`. Use a single warm light mark, preferably `#F4F0E6`, with the lime `#D6E27A` reserved for secondary applications rather than placed inside the icon. This keeps the app icon premium and prevents the mark from becoming a multi-color illustration.

**Tests before integration:** black mark on white, white mark on black, the icon at 1024, 256, 64, 32, and 16 px, and next to neutral marketplace icons. Reject it if the notch disappears, if it reads as a fruit with a stem, or if it resembles Apple’s bite too closely.

## What not to do next

Do not generate another batch of unrelated AI concepts. Do not add fashion objects, shopping bags, hangers, tags, eyes, stars, arrows, loops, or decorative accents. Do not use a gradient to compensate for a weak silhouette. Do not integrate a preview into the repository until the one-color silhouette passes the small-size test.
